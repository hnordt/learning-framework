# Learning Framework

A personal framework for understanding how I learn and deciding what to do when I get stuck.

## Why I am building this

I love learning. Using AI has made me even more interested in understanding things deeply. Being able to delegate lower-level tasks gives me more time to think about higher-level questions: what something means, how it works, and how it connects to what I already know.

This became especially clear when I started learning Rust. Along the way, I encountered computer science concepts that I wanted to review or understand more thoroughly. That led me back to mathematics.

I realized that revisiting some basic mathematical concepts could help me understand computer science better. Some were concepts I had overlooked when I was younger. Others were rules or models I had accepted and learned to use without checking whether I really understood them.

As I studied mathematics again, I became interested in the learning process itself. Sometimes, when a topic is difficult, I interpret that difficulty as evidence that I am bad at learning it. I want to become better at identifying what is actually causing the difficulty. Perhaps I am missing a prerequisite, have misunderstood a term, or have not yet understood how familiar ideas connect.

That is why I am building this framework. I want a small, practical model of thinking and learning that helps me identify a specific obstacle and choose a useful next step.

## Goal

The goal is to understand and improve my learning process.

I want this framework to help me:

- Make unfamiliar ideas easier to examine.
- Identify missing prerequisites and unclear relationships.
- Distinguish knowing a rule from understanding and applying it.
- Replace broad judgments about my ability with specific questions I can investigate.
- Decide when I understand enough to continue and when I need to review something.

The framework should help me return to the subject I am learning. Its usefulness will depend on whether it helps me make progress in practice.

## Scope and non-goals

I am interested in the functional level: what thinking and learning involve, and how that understanding can guide my study.

The following questions are outside the scope of this project:

- Philosophical explanations of why we think, what consciousness is, or what ultimately gives thoughts meaning.
- Biological explanations of how neurons work or how brain activity makes thinking possible.
- Finding an indivisible unit of thought or a complete theory of the mind.
- Designing a universal learning method that should work for everyone.

These are working definitions for a personal framework. I will refine them when doing so helps me understand or learn something more effectively.

## Starting model

I use two terms:

- **Mental representation:** information about something that the mind can work with, including its properties, relationships, and possible changes.
- **Mental process:** an activity through which the mind forms, retrieves, maintains, transforms, or uses mental representations.

A representation does not have to be a mental image. I can think about a red apple, a number, a rule, or a relationship without visualizing it.

For this framework, I do not need to treat "content" as a separate category. I use "representation" for the information I can think about.

| Example | Representation | Mental process |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recalling what an even number is | The concept of an even number | Retrieving its meaning |
| Working out whether 8 is even | Eight and the condition for being even | Applying the condition |
| Explaining why the sum of two even integers is even | Even integers, addition, and their relationships | Reasoning and explaining |

**Learning** is a process through which experience produces relatively lasting changes in knowledge or abilities. Within this model, learning can change both what I can represent and what I can do with those representations.

## Using the model when I get stuck

This is my initial approach:

1. **Name the target.** What specific idea, relationship, or operation am I trying to understand?
2. **Locate the gap.** Which term, prerequisite, connection, or step can I not explain or carry out?
3. **Work on that gap.** Review the relevant prerequisite, try a concrete example, use another explanation, or write out the steps to reduce how much I need to keep in mind.
4. **Return to the original task.** Reconnect the pieces and try to explain the idea or apply it to a new example.

A missing prerequisite is one possible explanation for difficulty. Other possibilities include an unclear explanation, unfamiliar notation, insufficient practice, or difficulty coordinating several ideas at once. The framework should help me investigate these possibilities without assuming the answer in advance.

Breaking a problem into smaller parts is useful when it reveals a gap or makes the work manageable. Understanding the original problem also requires putting those parts back together.

My practical stopping point is whether I can explain the idea and use it correctly in a new example at the level required by the current task. I can deepen that understanding later.

## How this framework will evolve

I will refine it through examples from mathematics, computer science, Rust, and other subjects I study. I will add a distinction or step when it helps resolve a real learning difficulty, and revise it when it does not.

The framework itself should remain small enough to use while learning.
